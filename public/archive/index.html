    How to read a morning report

    The upper table lists changes in status — a man moving from duty to hospital, a promotion, a transfer — each with a serial number, name, grade, and a code. The block below it records the unit's strength that day. The handwritten paragraph in the middle, Record of Events, is the only free text on the form, and it is where the war shows up.

    Checking the serial numbers

    The Archives hold a punch card for nearly every man who entered the Army between 1938 and 1946, keyed on his serial number. Every serial read off this film was looked up in it. Most land on a card bearing the same name. Sixty-two do not, and for those the man himself is on a card one or two digits away — which says which digit was misread. None of it has been applied: the card file has an error rate of its own, and a sixth of the cards were lost before the Archives converted them, so a missing card means nothing. The findings are held as questions to put back to the film.

    On uncertainty

    These are carbon copies of pencil, photographed onto microfilm eighty years ago. Where a reading is uncertain it is marked as such rather than smoothed over.
