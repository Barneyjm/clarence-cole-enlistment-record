# clarence-cole-enlistment-record

Museum-style site for the WWII service record of Battery C, 153rd Field Artillery Battalion.
Nuxt 4 + Tailwind 4 + Bun, statically generated. Not part of the CMDIY ecosystem — no Supabase,
no daisyUI, no Font Awesome. Plain Tailwind with CSS custom properties.

## Load-bearing invariants

**`data/morning-reports.jsonl` is the source of truth and the only file to hand-edit.**
Everything in `app/data/` is generated by `scripts/build-data.mjs` and git-ignored. Never edit
`app/data/*.json` directly; it will be overwritten on the next `bun run dev|build|generate`.

**Historical accuracy outranks completeness.** This site makes claims about real people who
died. Every quoted line must be traceable to a specific film page. If a reading is uncertain,
say so in the data (`"entry partly illegible on film"`) rather than guessing. Never fill a gap
from a secondary history — the whole premise is single-source fidelity to the original cards.

**Corrections are content, not noise.** The battery filed corrections constantly, sometimes
retracting entire entries months later. These are preserved verbatim in `events` rather than
silently applied, because they show the conditions the record was written under. Do not
"clean them up".

**Gazetteer matching is whole-word, longest-match-first.** See `placeFor()` in
`scripts/build-data.mjs`. This exists because a naive `includes()` made `"Ger"` (Ger, Manche)
match every station string ending `(Germany)`, silently relocating three months of the war to
Normandy. If you add a short place name, verify the stop list afterward.

**Two dates in the early Normandy period have no place name in the station field** — the
clerk wrote only "APO 230 France" and named the position in the record of events. These are
resolved by `gazetteer.json` → `overrides`, keyed by date range, each with a `why`.

**Serial numbers are probable, not authoritative.** Transcribed from microfilm; several are
visibly corrected by the battery itself in later entries. The roster deliberately keys on
serial where present, so a misread digit produces a duplicate person (e.g. Bachelder appears
twice). That is a faithful artifact of the source — do not merge them by name-similarity.

## Transcription status

Pages 1–218 of 284 are transcribed (397 daily reports, 2 May 1944 – 12 July 1945). Pages
219–284 cover mid-July to 10 October 1945: occupation, dissolution of the battery, and the
Calas staging area near Marseille. The source PDF is a 63 MB scan of microfilm; render with
`pdftoppm -jpeg -r 220`, then crop `1819x2210+0+200` per page to isolate the two cards. The
film drifts horizontally, so a full-width crop is required — a tighter crop clips left cards.

## On Sergeant Cole

He does not appear by name in any transcribed page. This is expected: morning reports name a
soldier only on a *status change*. Do not add speculative first-person narration or imply the
record documents his individual days. The site's framing — the battery's war as the frame his
service sits inside — is deliberate and should be preserved in any rewrite.
